# Design the product for multiple themes from the outset

On 2026-09-06, the product owner replaced the previous single-theme-first design constraint: Blueprint must support distinct thematic presentations across the 3D home and the rest of the Web and extension experience from the initial product design. Commercial product quality is the acceptance standard; this accepts the additional design and verification work instead of deferring theme extensibility until after a single-theme implementation.

The owner subsequently accepted the recommended first release of two complete themes: cyberpunk and Eastern landscape/wuxia. Themes may change characters, environments, materials, typography, panel shapes, motion, and limited composition, but not goal meanings, evidence criteria, confirmation, or navigation outcomes. The same user and achievements persist across different thematic avatars; a universal character mesh or wardrobe is not required. This accepts the cost of verifying the same journey twice to establish a genuine extension boundary, rather than treating a recolor as a second theme.

This decision supersedes the single-theme constraint recorded on 2026-08-26, not the factual description of the current M1 implementation. The [design review](../commercial-design-review.md) records the public example, progress-led home, and premium stylized asset direction. In the second round, the owner selected open assets first with no commercial asset purchases for now, one refined base avatar per theme with a few appearance presets, and initially private text/link evidence. Asset suitability, adaptation effort, and performance still require verification; free acquisition does not waive the quality bar. This does not authorize purchases, an immediate paid launch, or mark M1 complete.
